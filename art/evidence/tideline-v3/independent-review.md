## Sealed air chamber — preceding six-frame revision

The revision substantially improves the intended reading. The thick arch edges, continuous dark panel rails, side-pane boundaries, and clearer warm roadway now establish a glazed tunnel more convincingly. Frames 02 and 04 give the strongest impression of a solid road inside an enclosure, with water beyond it. The blue exterior tanks and warmer illuminated interior also help separate the two spaces.

I see no definite water surface crossing the road, floating interior debris, or other direct evidence that water occupies the chamber. Caustics on the floor can reasonably be light transmitted through the surrounding water and glass; they are not by themselves proof of an internally flooded tunnel.

There is still residual ambiguity. Frame 05 retains a pervasive dark cyan cast and a broad, bright caustic pattern across almost the entire foreground road, so it can still read as a submerged road beneath a skeletal canopy on a quick glance. Frame 08 has a similar, weaker ambiguity. In frame 04, the abrupt clear strip through the rippled overhead region makes the water/roof boundary look artificial. Some distant roof members in 02/08 visually kink or end, which weakens the continuous sealed-shell impression.

**Assessment: qualified improvement, not an unconditional pass on immediate sealed-air readability.** I can now reasonably interpret all four as enclosed underwater roads, particularly after seeing the panel construction, but I would not promise that a new player will instantly infer an air-filled chamber from every still. This review does not establish how glass, water, or phase transitions read in motion.

## Gantry reference/model match — prior visual conclusion retained

**Pass for the requested five shared visible details.** Both halves visibly share:

1. Twin weathered olive rectangular pillars on flared concrete feet.
2. An overhead beam with repeated triangular lattice bracing.
3. A narrow external ladder up the left pillar.
4. Four hanging cage lamps, three amber and one dark.

The game housing is larger relative to its pillar, the truss is heavier and denser, and its paint uses more regular vertical streaks than the reference's mottled wear. These are asset geometry/paint differences. The reference's fog, surrounding landscape, and weathered road differ from the comparison render's flat gray background, repeated ground, and broad bright stripe; those are scene differences and do not invalidate the five-detail match. The muted, painted asset treatment supports the intended PS2-era character, but matching five features does not establish full reference ambience parity.
