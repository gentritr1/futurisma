# Tideline V4 — independent sky turntable review

I visually inspected every supplied image from `sky-turntable/view-00.png` through `view-23.png`, in order. I did not inspect implementation, previous reports, or numerical metrics. This review concerns the visible 360° chase turntable; it does not establish how the sky behaves during a moving race.

**Verdict: pass for one continuous lighting state, no visible moon, and no conspicuously pasted horizon; qualified acceptance of the specific blue-hour color target.**

The sky keeps the same pale slate-gray cloud cover around the full turn. The industrial silhouettes remain in a consistent low band of haze. There is no direction with a bright sunset disk, a moon, stars, or a separate dark night sky. The transition back from view 23 to view 00 also looks consistent. I see no hard warm/night hemisphere split.

Warm road and structural lighting remains present throughout the turn. The brighter amber pavement in views 09–18 reads as the same environment's foreground lighting, rather than another time of day. Warm artificial lighting can coexist with a cool evening sky, so this contrast is not itself a continuity defect.

The reservation is that the sky is relatively pale and neutral, the horizon haze is brown, and amber dominates much of the foreground. My unprompted description would be **overcast industrial dusk**, rather than unmistakable blue hour. It is a coherent atmosphere, but I would not give an unconditional pass if the acceptance condition requires a clearly blue evening world on first sight. A cooler ambient impression in the haze and unlit surfaces would make that intention more legible while allowing the amber road lights to remain warm.

The distant refinery is softly integrated into the haze without a conspicuous cutout edge against the clouds. In the open side views, particularly 04–09, its repeated tank shapes and fairly uniform silhouette depth do have a painted-backdrop quality. That remains compatible with the intended older-game aesthetic; I did not see a glaring pasted-image boundary or mismatched sky direction. The thin vertical pale streaks visible in some views are too subtle and interrupted to establish a panorama seam from these stills.

One separate scene issue is visible around views 16–21: much of the nearby water becomes nearly black, with some straight-edged turquoise patches. That makes the water plane less convincing than in views 03–11. It is below the skyline and does not look like a sky/horizon join, but it is worth distinguishing from a fully seamless overall environment.

Only this review file was written. No implementation, other evidence, or tests were changed.
