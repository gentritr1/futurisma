# Tideline V4 devices — current independent visual review

I inspected only `surge-hero-versus-model.png` and `shield-hero-versus-model.png`. I did not inspect source, manifests, or implementation reports. These are judgments of the visible reference/model pairs, not of animation or readability during a race.

This review covers the **current 3340-triangle capture produced by `devices.mjs`**. The triangle count and capture-script name were supplied by the coordinating agent; they are contextual identifiers, not facts established by visual inspection. I re-opened both current PNGs for this revision. This text supersedes my previous device review.

## Surge

**Pass: five concrete shared details are visible.**

1. A large exposed six-blade rotor with broad gray blades arranged around a round central hub.
2. A thick circular outer flange with regularly spaced fasteners around its face.
3. A small round gauge with a needle at the upper left of the rotor.
4. A rectangular `S-07` identification plate at the upper right, with corner fasteners.
5. An amber cylindrical beacon inside a protective cage on the top-right corner.

The surrounding open rectangular frame, base, and projecting side couplings also preserve the reference's recognizable pump assembly silhouette. It reads as repurposed industrial machinery, and the rotor gives it a clear mechanical identity.

The current frame and base have visible edge bevels, and the projecting side barrels have rounded shading. These support the assembly's manufactured construction. The reference still has a more deeply layered hub and flange, substantial inner machinery, and darker separation between components. The model uses larger simplified fasteners and gives the gauge relatively little contrast. The reference beacon has a rounded cage top and a bright internal light; the model cage remains open and square-ended, and its amber lens reads more as a colored solid in this still.

## Shield

**Pass: five concrete shared details are visible.**

1. A closed circular iris made from overlapping shutter segments that converge toward the center.
2. A broad circular flange with an evenly spaced ring of visible fasteners.
3. A rectangular `P-12` plate in the upper-left portion of the housing, with corner fasteners.
4. A pale blue/cyan beacon within a protective cage on the top-right corner.
5. A broad upright rectangular housing supported by a projecting rectangular base.

The iris differentiates the shield clearly from Surge's open fan. Together with the flanged opening, service label, beacon, and side mechanical forms, it retains the salvaged pump-works fiction and the main reference composition.

The current housing has visible beveled edges and a recessed panel behind the circular mechanism. The reference's iris still has a more tightly fitted aperture with curved metal leaves. The model uses broader, flatter polygonal pieces, so it reads more like a mechanical cover than the reference's deep iris assembly. The side actuators retain broad simplified silhouettes and much less visible cylinder-and-piston detail. The beacon has substantially less luminous presence than the reference in this still.

## Shared qualification

The lower polygon count and painted texture treatment suit the intended remembered-PS2 direction. Both assemblies remain identifiable from their references and pass the requested five-detail test.

The revised base plinths now read clearly as cracked concrete, with clean beveled edges. My earlier wood-like-base criticism is resolved in these captures. Concrete is a plausible foundation for salvaged pump equipment, although it is a visible material departure from the references' metal bases.

The remaining material qualification is on the machinery itself. The references have distinctive chipped sheet steel, exposed edges, scratches, and rust. The models' relatively uniform mottled gray-brown finish suggests rough cast material and can still look somewhat stone-like, particularly across the broad shield leaves and frame corner plates. The visible circular mechanisms, fasteners, barrels, gauge, labels, and protective beacon cages nevertheless keep both assemblies readable as industrial hardware. Layered mechanical depth and beacon brightness remain more simplified than the references.

**Current acceptance: both pass the five-detail condition and retain the salvaged pump-works identity, with the material and shape qualifications above.** This is acceptance of the intended stylized game assets, not a claim of close material or geometric parity with the hero renders.

The reference halves use atmospheric studio backgrounds and grounded contact shadows; the model halves use a plain gray background. Those scene differences are separate from the geometry and material observations above. These images establish recognizable matching hardware, with qualified material fidelity; they do not establish in-game placement, response animation, or signal visibility at racing speed.
