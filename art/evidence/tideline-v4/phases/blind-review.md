**Acceptance result: the requirement for all ten classifications at at least 90% confidence is not met.** Seven images reach that confidence threshold; A, C, and I do not. I cannot establish a 10/10 correctness score without a separately checked answer key, and I have not read one.

The strong cues are water above the glazed roof for full flooding and a visible horizontal waterline partway up the enclosure for an intermediate phase. Those are direct structural comparisons. The ambiguous port and approach views mostly show a dry road, permanent-looking harbor water, and partly occluded foundation bands. A dry road alone cannot distinguish the global phase, since the road can remain dry within a flooded environment.

The dark bands on buildings are useful weathering but do not intrinsically tell a new viewer which band corresponds to a full, intermediate, or drained state. The most helpful visual addition would be a clearly exposed and consistent water-level reference visible from the road in each port composition. Color changes alone would leave the same ambiguity.

The flooded chamber images read as warm solid road within a glazed enclosure, with water outside; I see no definite internal water surface. This phase review is based on still images and does not assess whether an animated tide change makes the ambiguous states clear in play.
